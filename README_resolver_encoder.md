# Resolver → Tamagawa OIH48-2500P8-L6-5V encoder emulator

Teensy 4.0 demo board, revision B (2026-11-13): parts chosen from LCSC stock

This board reads a brushless resolver and outputs the same signals as a Tamagawa OIH48-2500P8-L6-5V incremental encoder, so a servo drive (Kinco FD144S) can use a resolver-equipped motor as if it had the original encoder.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Resolver | Tamagawa Smartsyn TS2650N562E210 (datasheet not found, see Open items) |
| Output | A, B, Z, U, V, W, each as an RS-422 differential pair (AM26C31, 5 V) |
| Resolution | 2500 lines/rev (10,000 counts/rev after ×4) |
| Commutation | U/V/W for an 8-pole motor (4 cycles/rev, edges every 15° mechanical) |
| Resolver-to-digital | AD2S1210, serial (SPI) mode, 14-bit default, 16-bit selectable |
| Controller | Teensy 4.0 (plug-in module) |
| Supply | 24 VDC from the cabinet, galvanically isolated on the board |
| Service port | Isolated RS-485 module, automatic direction, max 115,200 bps, daisy chain |

## 3. Circuit description

### 3.1 Power (`power.kicad_sch`)

| Rail | Source | Feeds | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `24V_IN` / `COM` | J1 | Input protection | Primary side. `COM` is **not** connected to `GND` |
| `12V` | URB2412S-6WR3, YLPTEC (isolated, 6 W) | Everything below | 2 × 47 µF in, 22 µF out, NFE31PT, then 10 µH + 22 µF + 47 µF |
| `5V_BUCK` | TPS62160, 105 k / 20 k → 5.0 V | Teensy VIN, AM26C31, SRV05 | 2.2 µH, 22 µF out |
| `5VCCA` | LP2985A-50 behind a 75 Ω / 1 W pre-drop | AD2S1210 AVDD + DVDD, AMS1117, clamp diodes | Enabled by Teensy (`5VCCA_EN`, 100 k pull-down) |
| `VDRIVE_3V3` | AMS1117-3.3 from `5VCCA` | AD2S1210 VDRIVE and its pull-ups | 470 Ω dummy load + LED, 22 µF tantalum out |
| `3V3_MCU` | Teensy's own regulator | TD301D485H-A (40 mA idle, 70 mA max) | Keep separate from `VDRIVE_3V3` |
| `GND_485` | Inside the TD301D485H-A | RS-485 bus reference | Isolated from `GND` |

Points that matter:

- **Supply order for the AD2S1210.** AVDD and DVDD share one regulator, so they always track. VDRIVE is derived from 5VCCA, so it cannot rise first. D5 (RB520S30, anode on `VDRIVE_3V3`, cathode on `5VCCA`) keeps VDRIVE within 0.3 V of AVDD at power-down.
- **R4 (75 Ω, 2512).** It drops about 4.9 V at 65 mA, so the SOT-23-5 LDO dissipates only about 0.15 W. With C13/C14 it also forms a ~65 Hz RC pre-filter.
- **The AMS1117 needs load and ESR.** R6 (470 Ω) plus the LED give about 10 mA. C21 is a 22 µF tantalum (ESR about 3 Ω).
- **Isolation.** The URB2412S-6WR3 is rated 1600 VDC. Keep ≥ 4 mm between `COM`/24 V copper and `GND` copper, with no copper pour under the module.

### 3.2 Resolver-to-digital converter (`rdc.kicad_sch`)

- AD2S1210 in **serial mode**. `SOE` has a 10 k pull-down; the Teensy can still drive it through 1 k.
- `CS` is tied low through 1 k. `WR/FSYNC` (net `SYNC`) frames each SPI transfer.
- `RD` is pulled up to `VDRIVE_3V3` (required in serial mode).
- `RESET` has a 4.7 k pull-down, so the chip stays in reset until the Teensy releases it. 4.7 k is needed because the pin has an internal pull-up of about 80 µA.
- `A0` and `A1` are tied together (net `A0A1`): low = read position, high = configuration mode. There is no external pull resistor; the internal pull-ups hold configuration mode during boot.
- `RES1` is fixed high. `RES0` comes from the Teensy: **0 = 14-bit, 1 = 16-bit**. The control register must be written with the same resolution.
- Clock: **8 MHz** crystal (load capacitance 20 pF) with 2 × 27 pF. Excitation frequency = code × f_clk / 2¹⁵, so **code 40 = 9.77 kHz**, which is valid in both 14-bit (3–12 kHz) and 16-bit (2–10 kHz) mode.
- DB0–DB12, A, B, NM and DIR are not connected.

### 3.3 Excitation buffer (`resolver_if.kicad_sch`)

Single-stage bridge buffer with an AD8397, after Analog Devices CN-0276.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Gain per side (24.9 k / 10 k) | 2.49, inverting |
| Output per side | 8.0 / 9.0 / 10.0 Vpp (for AD2S1210 EXC of 3.2 / 3.6 / 4.0 Vpp) |
| Differential across R1–R2 | ≈ 18 Vpp ≈ 6.4 Vrms typical |
| `VREF_EXC` (24.9 k / 10 k from 12 V) | 3.44 V |
| Output DC level | 3.44 × 3.49 − 2.49 × 2.47 ≈ 5.85 V (close to mid-rail) |
| Feedback pole (24.9 k, 120 pF) | ≈ 53 kHz, about 11° lag at 10 kHz |

If you change the gain G, recompute `VREF_EXC = (6 + 2.47 × G) / (1 + G)`.

Supply: 10 µH from 12 V, then 100 nF + 10 µF directly at +VS, plus a damping branch of 1 Ω + 22 µF. The exposed pad goes to GND.

### 3.4 SIN/COS attenuator and ratio jumpers

Each resolver output passes through 10.0 k series resistors, with 10.7 k from each leg to `REFOUT` (2.47 V bias). A jumper adds a resistor **across** the pair to change the attenuation.

| JP1 (SIN) and JP2 (COS), same position on both | Attenuation | Resolver transformation ratio | Level at AD2S1210 (typical) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open | 0.517 | 0.28 – 0.36 | 2.6 – 3.3 Vpp |
| 1–2 (51.1 k) | 0.430 | 0.36 – 0.43 | 2.8 – 3.3 Vpp |
| 2–3 (24.0 k) | 0.361 | 0.43 – 0.50 | 2.8 – 3.2 Vpp |

The AD2S1210 accepts 2.3–4.0 Vpp (3.15 Vpp nominal). **Start with 1–2 on both jumpers** for the TS2650N562E210: the previous circuit worked with 8 Vpp excitation and no attenuation, which suggests a ratio near 0.4. Then measure SIN–SINLO with a scope.

Each input has a BAT54S clamp to `5VCCA` and `GND`, and 100 pF across each pair.

### 3.5 Controller and encoder outputs (`mcu_encoder.kicad_sch`)

- The Teensy generates A/B/Z/U/V/W on pins 14–19. These six pins are on one fast GPIO port (GPIO6), so one register write changes them together.
- Each line goes through 33 Ω to an AM26C31. `G` is tied to 5 V and `G̅` to GND, so the outputs are always enabled. Unused inputs of U8 are grounded.
- Three SRV05-4 arrays protect the 12 output lines.
- J3 carries A, B, Z and `GND`. J4 carries U, V, W and the shield.
- **Board temperature (motor overheat).** RT1 (10 k NTC, 0603) from `3V3_MCU` to `CAL_BTN` and R43 (3.3 k) from `CAL_BTN` to `GND` form a divider on Teensy pin 23 / A9, the same pin as the CAL button. Every Teensy I/O pin was already in use, so the sensor shares this one: the pin reads the temperature voltage normally and 0 V while the button is pressed. RT1 sits on the front next to mounting hole H4 (bottom left), so it follows the temperature of the motor end bell through the screw and the board. It measures the board, not the winding; a winding runs hotter, so set the trip point by test.

### 3.6 RS-485 (`rs485.kicad_sch`)

- TD301D485H-A (YLPTEC): a DIP module with the transceiver, signal isolation and an isolated supply inside. It needs only 3.3 V, TXD and RXD. Direction switching is automatic.
- **Maximum speed is 115,200 bps** and up to 32 nodes, according to two datasheets for this part number. The LCSC listing says 500 kbps; do not rely on that.
- The module has built-in 4.7–5.1 kΩ pull-up and pull-down resistors on A and B. That bias is weak, so the termination is AC-coupled: 120 Ω in series with 10 nF, enabled by JP3. A plain 120 Ω at both ends would pull the idle level below the receiver threshold.
- SM712 TVS across A, B and `GND_485`.
- Two connectors (IN and OUT) are wired straight through for a daisy chain.

## 6. Firmware notes

### Start-up sequence

1. Set every pin that goes to the AD2S1210 low or as input (`SYNC`, `SAMPLE`, `A0A1`, `RES0`, `SOE_MCU`, `RD_MCU`, `RESET`, SPI pins). A pin driven high into the unpowered chip would back-feed it.
2. Drive `5VCCA_EN` high. Wait about 10 ms for 5VCCA and VDRIVE.
3. Set `RES0` (0 = 14-bit), `SOE_MCU` low, `RD_MCU` high.
4. Release `RESET` (high). Wait a few ms.
5. Set `A0A1` high (configuration mode). Write the excitation frequency (code 40), the control register (resolution matching `RES0`), and the LOS/DOS thresholds.
6. Set `A0A1` low (position mode). Wait 20–50 ms until `LOT` and `DOS` are high.
7. Read the absolute angle, preload the output counter, then enable the encoder outputs.

### Position read (serial mode)

Pulse `SAMPLE` low, wait at least 6 clock periods + 20 ns (about 0.8 µs at 8 MHz), pull `SYNC` low, clock out 16 bits (24 bits to include the fault byte), release `SYNC`. SPI clock up to 25 MHz with VDRIVE at 3.3 V. Check the SPI mode against the AD2S1210 timing diagram.

### Scaling to 2500 lines

The position is read over SPI as one 16-bit word, MSB first. The word is **left-justified**: in 14-bit mode the angle is in bits 15…2 and bits 1…0 read as zero. Scale the raw SPI word directly, so the same formula works in both modes:

| Mode | Raw SPI word | Output position (0…9999) |
|---|---|---|
| 14-bit | 0…65532, steps of 4 | `(word16 × 625) >> 12` |
| 16-bit | 0…65535 | `(word16 × 625) >> 12` |

Do not apply a 14-bit formula such as `(x × 625) >> 10` to the raw word; that needs `x = word16 >> 2` first.

- Add the stored commutation offset, modulo 10000.
- A/B: Gray code of the two low bits. Z: high for counts 0–3 (one A period).
- U/V/W: 24 edges per revolution; threshold k = round(k × 10000 / 24). Six-step sequence 101 → 100 → 110 → 010 → 011 → 001 (verify against the original encoder on a scope).
- Sample at 40–100 kHz and spread the counts evenly over each sample period, stepping one count at a time, so the drive sees evenly spaced edges.
- Use a 10000-entry table that holds the GPIO6 bit pattern of all six outputs for each count, and write it in one operation.
- If the resolver is not 1X, Z and absolute U/V/W need extra handling (see Open items).

### Mode choice

Use **14-bit** for servo feedback: tracking bandwidth is about four times higher than in 16-bit, and 16384 counts already exceed the 10000 needed. 16-bit only helps for slow, high-resolution applications. Change resolution only through a full restart sequence.

### Temperature and CAL button (pin 23 / A9)

Leave the pin as a plain analog input (`pinMode(23, INPUT_DISABLE)`), no pull-up. `V = 3.3 V × 3.3k / (3.3k + R_NTC)`, so the voltage rises with temperature:

| Temperature | R_NTC | V at A9 |
|---|---|---|
| −20 °C | 68 k | 0.15 V |
| 0 °C | 27 k | 0.36 V |
| 25 °C | 10 k | 0.82 V |
| 60 °C | 3.0 k | 1.73 V |
| 85 °C | 1.45 k | 2.29 V |
| 105 °C | 0.86 k | 2.62 V |
| 125 °C | 0.54 k | 2.83 V |

Values are for the Murata NCP18XH103F03RB (B25/50 = 3380 K, B25/100 = 3455 K); use the datasheet table or the B equation for other points. A reading below about 0.05 V means the button is pressed (the switch shorts the pin to GND); ignore the temperature while it is. Below about −30 °C the two cannot be told apart. C45 (100 nF) already filters the node; average a few samples. The Teensy also has an internal die sensor (`tempmonGetTemp()`) as a cross-check.

### RS-485

`Serial2.begin(115200);` No direction pin is needed. 115,200 bps is the module's limit.

## 7. Bring-up checklist

1. Before fitting the Teensy and AD2S1210 (or with `5VCCA_EN` held low): apply 24 V and check `12V`, `5V_BUCK` = 5.0 V.
2. Pull `5VCCA_EN` high by hand: check `5VCCA` = 5.0 V and `VDRIVE_3V3` = 3.3 V, and that VDRIVE never leads 5VCCA at power-up or power-down (scope, two channels).
3. Check `VREF_EXC` ≈ 3.44 V and both AD8397 outputs ≈ 5.9 V DC with no excitation.
4. With the AD2S1210 running: R1–R2 should show a sine of about 18 Vpp at 9.77 kHz with no clipping.
5. With the resolver connected: SIN–SINLO and COS–COSLO at the AD2S1210 should peak at 2.6–3.5 Vpp. If not, move JP1/JP2.
6. `LOT` and `DOS` high. Turn the shaft one revolution and check the angle goes through one full cycle (this confirms a 1X resolver).
7. Check A/B/Z/U/V/W on J3 and J4 against the original encoder's timing, then calibrate the commutation offset (lock the rotor with DC current U → V+W, read the angle, store it).
8. RS-485: fit JP3 on the two end nodes and check that the idle A–B voltage is positive. Test at 115,200 bps over the full cable length.

## 8. Open items

1. **Resolver data.** No datasheet was found for TS2650N562E210. Confirm from the label or by measurement: transformation ratio, rated excitation voltage and frequency, and **speed (1X or multi-speed)**. A sibling part (TS2650N11E78) is 1X, ratio 0.30, max 10 Vrms, max 4 kHz. If your variant is also limited to about 4 kHz, use excitation code 16 (3.9 kHz) and 14-bit mode.
2. **AD2S1210 pins 38 and 39.** The symbol calls pin 38 `EXC` and pin 39 `EXC_N`. Check the datasheet; swapping them only inverts the excitation phase, and the two buffer channels are symmetric.
3. **RS-485 speed.** The TD301D485H-A is limited to 115,200 bps. If you need more, the in-stock alternative is the CMT83085 (LCSC C32713218, 12 Mbps, SOIC-16 wide). It needs a DE pin from the Teensy and a separate isolated supply, so it is a schematic change.
4. **Clone modules.** PS1 and U9 are YLPTEC parts sold under MORNSUN part numbers. The schematic uses the MORNSUN pinouts (PS1: 1 GND, 2 Vin, 3 Ctrl, 6 +Vo, 7 0V; U9: 1 VCC, 2 GND, 3 TXD, 4 RXD, 8 B, 9 A, 10 RGND). Check both against the YLPTEC datasheets before layout.
5. **Drive encoder supply.** The board is powered from 24 V. Confirm the FD144S does not need to see current on its +5 V encoder pin, and check its encoder connector pinout.
6. **Power-on timing with the drive.** If the drive powers up before this board, it may raise an encoder alarm.
7. **Clock.** 8 MHz works. An 8.192 MHz crystal is in stock (C41428618, load 12 pF, so use 18 pF capacitors) and matches the datasheet tables. No 10.24 MHz 3225 crystal was found on LCSC.
8. **Isolation rating.** PS1 is 1600 VDC and U9 is 2500 V. A 3000 VDC supply module needs a different footprint.
9. **AMS1117 stability** with the chosen output capacitor: scope `VDRIVE_3V3` for oscillation. Order the SOT-223 part (C6186); C20628883 is SOT-89.
10. **Common-mode choke (L1).** L1 is now the SMD TDK ACM7060-701-2PL (7 × 6 mm, 700 Ω at 100 MHz, 4 A) instead of the 5 mH through-hole UU9.8. Two things to check: (a) its windings are pins 1–4 and 2–3, and the schematic is wired that way; confirm with a continuity test before first power-up, because a part with windings 1–2 / 4–3 would short the 24 V input. (b) It has far less inductance than the 5 mH part, so it filters much less below about 1 MHz. The module datasheet suggests 470 µH; if conducted emissions matter, a 470 µH SMD choke of about 9 × 6 mm (for example PROD Tech PSCLAQ0905-471Y, LCSC C2829005) is the alternative, with a different footprint.

## 10. PCB: board, placement and routing

`resolver_encoder.kicad_pcb` is placed and routed. KiCad 10 DRC with schematic parity reports **0 unconnected items and no clearance, track-width, edge-clearance or courtyard errors**. What remains is cosmetic: silkscreen overlaps (the reference texts were never tidied), "missing MPN field" parity warnings (cleared by *Update PCB from Schematic*), and five parity errors for Teensy pads 15–19, which the socket footprint leaves out on purpose.

The routing is autorouted (Freerouting 2.5.0, run from the command line on the DSN export) and has **not** been reviewed by hand. See "How the board was routed" below before sending it out.

- **Board:** 64 × 64 mm, square corners, 6 copper layers. The board origin is the top-left corner at (100, 100) mm on the sheet.
- **Mounting holes:** four M3 (3.2 mm) holes, 3.0 mm in from each edge (58 × 58 mm pattern), with a 6 mm cap-screw head keepout. H1 (bottom right) is the plated PE hole; H2–H4 are unplated and exist only on the board. 3.0 mm is used because at 4 mm the bottom-right hole collides with J4.
- **Connectors overhang the board.** Only the solder pads are on the board: each pad row is 2.5 mm from the edge and the header bodies stick out about 5.5 mm. This leaves a free strip behind every connector for routing.
- **Around U4:** a 1.6 mm strip around U4 on the front is kept free of parts for pin fan-out. U4's decoupling capacitors, the crystal with its load capacitors and FL3 are on the back, directly under the chip; the pull-up and strap resistors sit outside the strip.
- **Around U5:** U5 (AD8397) and its parts are on the back, below the clamp diodes: feedback parts R16/C33 and R15 on its left, R18/C34 and R17 above right, supply capacitors C35/C36 with the damping branch R19/C37 on its right, and the reference divider R20/R21/C38/C39 in a row below L4/L5. The twelve attenuator resistors stay on the front in a 4 × 3 grid between J2 and U4, with the clamp diodes D6–D9 and filter capacitors C41, C42 behind them on the back.
- **12 V filter:** FL1 and L2 are on the front between L1, U9 and PS1, above the LED row. C7 (47 µF bulk) is on the front over U5.
- **Temperature sensor:** RT1 and R43 are on the front beside mounting hole H4, next to Teensy pads 30–32.
- **Sides:** 67 parts on the front, 76 on the back. All tall parts (terminal blocks, Teensy, PS1, U9, C2, C3, C7) are on the front.

`resolver_encoder.kicad_dru` relaxes the 2.0 mm to 0.2 mm between two nets of the same isolated domain (for example `24V_P` to `COM`). Minimum track width is 0.15 mm, because the autorouter necks tracks down at fine-pitch pins.

Measured on the routed board: 24 V side to everything else ≥ 2.2 mm, RS-485 bus to everything else ≥ 2.0 mm, `SHIELD` to logic ≥ 0.6 mm. The module ratings (PS1 1600 VDC, U9 2500 V) call for more than 2 mm if they are to be used in full; this placement cannot give more, because L1 sits 3.2 mm below J6 and U9's bus pins are 2.4 mm from D12. `SHIELD` was planned at 1.5 mm, but the link from J2 pin 7 to the PE hole H1 has to pass between the J2 pads and the vias beside them and only fits at 0.6 mm.

### Copper areas

One `GND` zone is filled on all six layers around the routing. Its outline leaves out the 24 V corner (x > 31.5 mm, y < 18.5 mm, and x > 41 mm down to y = 22.6 mm) and the RS-485 strip and corner (y < 5.6 mm left of x = 31.5 mm, and x, y < 17.3 mm), so there is no `GND` copper on any layer under the isolated parts. Signals are routed on all six layers, so no layer is an unbroken plane: In1 and In4 carry the fewest tracks (93 and 130 segments out of 1533) and are the closest thing to planes.

### How the board was routed

1. All previous routing was removed. The 24 V nets were autorouted alone with 2 mm keep-off from every other pad, then the RS-485 nets the same way.
2. Everything else was autorouted on six layers with the net-class widths and clearances above, and with the routing boundary pulled in 0.45 mm from the board edge.
3. Three connections the autorouter could not finish were added by script: a via from D6 pin 3 to the `SIN` track, the `5V_BUCK` sense connection of U1 pin 6 (the `SW_5V` track next to it was moved 0.55 mm), and the `SHIELD` link from J2 pin 7 to H1.
4. Seven tracks that were 2–17 µm under the 0.2 mm clearance were made 0.04 mm narrower.

Known weaknesses, to fix by hand if the board is built as is:

- **`SHIELD` crosses the board on In3**, from J6 down between U9 and L1 and diagonally to J2. A chassis net should not run through the middle of the analog area; connectors on all four edges leave no route around the outside. Consider connecting the four cable shields to the chassis at the connectors instead.
- **No solid ground plane.** Reference-plane quality under U4, the SIN/COS pairs and the buck converter is whatever the fills left over. Moving the inner-layer tracks off In1 would give a real plane; the autorouter could not complete the board with In1 reserved (about 30 connections stayed open).
- **`GND` and the supply rails are 0.3–0.4 mm tracks plus fills**, not pours. The 12 V and 5 V current is below 0.5 A, so this is adequate for copper heating, but the buck converter loop (U1, L3, C8–C10) was not laid out as a tight loop.
- **SIN/SINLO and COS/COSLO are not routed as pairs**, and the crystal tracks were not given any special treatment.

- **Parts under the Teensy** must stay lower than the 8.5 mm sockets. They are all 0603/0805/1206 parts.

### Layout guidance

- 6 layers, with a solid ground plane under the analog section (not achieved by the autorouted version, see above).
- Keep the TPS62160 input capacitors, inductor and output capacitor in a tight loop. Keep the SW node small and away from SIN/COS and the encoder lines.
- Put the decoupling capacitors of the AD2S1210 at pins 43, 6, 18 and 46.
- Route SIN/SINLO and COS/COSLO as matched pairs, away from the excitation outputs.
- Give the AD8397 exposed pad and the NFE31PT ground pads several vias to the plane.
- Put D5 between the VDRIVE and AVDD supply traces near the AD2S1210.
- Keep ≥ 4 mm creepage across PS1 and U9, with no copper under the isolation gap.
- Keep the RS-485 stub from the IN/OUT connectors to U9 short.
